StabilityGuard: a repair-first recovery ladder that kills the "stutter, then the video resumes but the audio is gone and the subtitles freeze at the stutter timestamp, then the old recovery restarts everything and it happens again" class - worst on 1-2GB Android TV boxes. Reliable playback, continuous audio, continuous subtitles, stable video and no unnecessary restarts - in that order, always above motion work.

Fixes
- L1 sync repair (never a reload): when the player state goes stale after a stutter (a paused flag that never got its clearing event, its mirror-image playing flag left stale, or the provider clock stream dying while the video keeps rendering) the guard re-requests authoritative state through the documented command surface (getPaused / getMuted / getPlaybackRate), clears stuck seeking and paused flags, and re-anchors the app clock at the real element position - one small command round-trip, playback untouched
- L2 audio continuity: if audio appears dead (muted under us without the viewer asking for it, the decoded-audio counter freezing while the picture advances, a stalled playback state or an audio session error on our own surface) the documented setMuted(false) nudge re-kicks audio - only when the viewer has not intentionally muted - and the playback rate is restored to the viewer's own pick, never an invented value. On the owned Media3 HLS surface the recovery is real: a controlled playWhenReady toggle plus seekTo(currentPosition) that preserves the position exactly
- L3 controlled kick: pause then play through documented commands about 600ms apart, position, tracks and settings untouched - rate-limited to once per 5 minutes
- L4 last resort: the existing reopen-at-position machinery and nothing else - the WebView render-process rebuild path reopens the episode at the live position (the owned HLS surface reopens through the same load path), budgeted at one per 15 minutes and at most 3 per hour. Nothing ever restarts from zero
- Audio focus and route handling: the app holds audio focus while playing (abandoned on stop, API-guarded) and watches headset, HDMI and audio-becoming-noisy changes; a focus regain after a loss or a route change while playing runs the audio nudge instead of leaving dead audio
- Subtitle auto-resync: the caption clock re-anchors to the authoritative position on every repair and the lookup is forced to repaint, so a cue can never stay pinned at the stutter timestamp - and a stale cue is cleared outright while the clock is stale instead of freezing on screen
- False-trigger suppression: the guard never acts on a user pause (the app's own pause toggle or a room pause), never unmutes against a user mute, never acts inside an in-flight seek or an episode/provider transition, and never reacts to a short stall - every observation-based signal must be sustained across ticks before any rung fires. The cooldowns (repair 20s, nudge 5min, kick 5min, restart 15min + max 3/hour) are enforced in the engine, not the UI
- Prevention first (L0): dropped-frame bursts, JS frame gaps and sustained event-loop lag demote FluidMotion and suspend decorative work before anything essential is touched
- FluidMotion yields to stability: from guard level 2 the motion engine stands its work down entirely (tier-D behavior) and only climbs back one step at a time after sustained recovery - interpolation and effects never cost audio or subtitles anything
- Three real settings in the Audio & Subtitles panel (default ON): "Stability protection" gates the L1-L4 ladder, "Audio continuity" the audio nudges plus focus/route recovery, "Subtitle auto-resync" the caption-clock re-anchoring - each gates its own subsystem, none is decorative
- Diagnostics are honest: the Motion row's status line now carries the live guard level, repair/nudge/kick/restart counts, the last real reason and the audio focus state - "Unavailable" where nothing is known

Honest limits
- Provider iframe decoders and their audio pipelines are not directly controllable. Every action goes through documented provider commands or state the app itself owns; where a provider surface does not answer a state query the guard falls back to the authoritative element read, and where that too is opaque the ladder can only work from position cadence
- A pause pressed inside a provider's own embed player is indistinguishable from a pause the source issued itself. The guard treats uncommanded pauses as suspect (as the web build does) but every action stays rate-limited - pause from the app's own bar to hold the ladder down completely
- On the owned Media3 surface, Android exposes no public audio-renderer-only rebuild: audio recovery there is the controlled position-preserving nudge rather than a renderer swap
- The decoded-audio "silent pipeline" detector reports Unavailable on surfaces that do not expose webkitAudioDecodedByteCount - it is never faked
- Physical TV verification on 1-2GB boxes is still pending; this build is verified at the code level (native QA locks and a clean Gradle build)
